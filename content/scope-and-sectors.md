# Scope and sectors

The initial scope covers the digital systems, data, people, procedures, and physical infrastructure that support biological research, health, biotechnology, and related policy and security functions.

## Sectors in scope

- Pharmaceutical companies
- Institutes of health
- Research facilities
- National defence
- Public health and laboratory networks
- Biotechnology, bioinformatics, and biomanufacturing organizations
- Pharmaceutical and biomedical organizations
- Biological data providers and outsourced service providers
- Veterinary and agricultural organizations
- Critical infrastructure supporting biological work

## Risk surface

The knowledge base should consider risks across the full lifecycle of biological work, including:

- Data generation, storage, analysis, and sharing
- Bioinformatics platforms, AI-enabled workflows, and digital dependencies
- Laboratory information management systems and connected instruments
- Cloud services, outsourced infrastructure, software, and supply chains
- Remote access and operational technology
- Personnel, training, and organizational processes
- Biological materials, protocols, and other sensitive knowledge
- Incident response, continuity, and recovery

## Topics requiring further review

The relationship between cyberbiosecurity and emerging concepts such as mirror life and synthetic life is not yet defined for this project. These topics should be assessed for their cyber, biosecurity, biosafety, governance, and responsible-use implications before they are included as established scope areas.

Other emerging topics for evidence review include biological data storage, biocryptography, and the security of sequencing and synthesis workflows. These should be addressed at a governance and risk-management level until their evidence base, relevance, and responsible-publication boundaries are established.

This list is a starting scope. It must be refined through the literature review and consultation with subject-matter experts.
